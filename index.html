    How the links were drawn

    Each line records a concrete connection: a text one author quotes from another, a teacher and student, a community that inherited a practice, or an argument one side had with another. Solid lines pass an idea on. Dashed lines mark a clash, where one side reacted against the other. Dotted lines are connections that scholars propose but still debate.

    Dates before the monarchy (Abraham to Joshua) follow the biblical tradition, which archaeology cannot confirm. Later dates are the usual scholarly estimates. The book of Daniel, for example, is placed in the Maccabean crisis around 165 BCE, where most historians date it, rather than in the Babylonian exile where its story is set.

    On the scripture map, a line means one text was copied, translated or revised from another, or written in reaction to it. The verse comparisons quote each version in its own language with an English gloss. Older English spellings are modernized where noted, and later revisions are named where a version changed over time.

    The maps show how ideas and texts travelled. They do not rule on which reading is right.
